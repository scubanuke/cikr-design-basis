A path through the examples

For defenders

These questions are framed from the defender's side and answered with reasoning and a pointer to the published example. They never rank targets, because a defender's question turned around is an attacker's.

This page is a guided path through material the site already shows. It is not regulatory guidance and not a compliance checklist.

Which of my assets would a capable adversary choose, and why?

The ones whose loss gives you the hardest recovery: the elements a careful reader of your own safety analysis would pick. That is what precision means. Look also for anything many of your systems share, because one action against a common component reaches every place it is used. Reading your own analysis this way is the exercise; this site does not do it for you. The facility class cards show how each sector frames it.

What must I hold on local resources when repair and outside support are denied, and for how long?

Whatever it takes to reach and hold a protected state, for as long as the adversary's action lasts. A plan whose safe condition depends on off-site power, resupply or outside repair meets the accident standard and fails this one. Each sector names its own protected state; see the sector framework and facility class cards.

Which other systems fail alongside mine?

Start with the two dependency lists on your sector's page, then read the use-case summary card. Most of the chosen use cases turn on another sector being down at the same time: communications with the grid denied, care delivery through a grid and communications outage, settlement through a campaign on both.

What does a real event in my sector say about my plan?

Read the reference-event card on your sector's page. It reads what happened back through the design basis and asks whether the method was enough, and its findings go back to revise the design. Then ask the same question of your own plan.
