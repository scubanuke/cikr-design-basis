A path through the examples

For supply chain

Supply chain cuts across sectors, so this page is a path through the tree rather than a branch of it.

This page is a guided path through material the site already shows. It is not regulatory guidance and not a compliance checklist.

How does a trusted product become a path of compromise?

See the information technology sector's facility class, Software, Hardware and Product Supply Chain, and its use case, A Capability Pre-Positioned in a Trusted Product (Information Technology).

What follows when an asset cannot be replaced quickly?

See Long-Lead Critical Components, its use case The Asset and Its Replacement Struck Together, and the reference event Denial of Large-Transformer Replacement in Ukraine (Critical Manufacturing).

What does a single-source dependency cost?

See the chemical sector's use case, The Sole Disinfectant Supplier Taken Down (Chemical), and read it beside the water framework, which will not let a utility count on chemical deliveries when their loss is one of the events planned for (Water and Wastewater).

What does a trusted update taking systems down teach?

See the reference event Faulty Content Update of 19 July 2024 (Information Technology). It involved no adversary. Its lesson is about shared components: what reaches every system that uses a component, by accident, can reach them all by design.

Where else should I look?

Outside this site, the SCRM Companion Agent describes a multi-agent approach to supply chain risk management in the bulk power system. A paper on a strategic reserve for high-voltage, long-lead equipment will be linked here when it is published.

Wanting more

Fuller use cases and more detailed versions are released to defenders, regulators and researchers on request. See Ask for more.
