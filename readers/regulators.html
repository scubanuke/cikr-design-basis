A path through the examples

For regulators

A regulator's questions turn on scope and on the boundary between the operator's duty and the government's. The answers point to the examples and go only as far as the deposited documents state.

This page is a guided path through material the site already shows. It is not regulatory guidance and not a compliance checklist.

What range of events should a facility be designed against?

Three layers, each wider than the one before: the design basis itself, the accidents experience has taught us to expect, and deliberate military action. Each sector framework states the range for its sector, and each facility class takes it down to one kind of facility. Start with the sector framework card on any sector page, and with What is a design basis?

Where does the accident layer end and the adversary layer begin?

At the four marks: intentionality, persistence, precision and denial. An actor is within the adversary layer if it shows all four at the level characterized in the facility's threat assessment. Identity informs that assessment but does not decide it. When a real event is read back, it counts as military action only if all four are established on the record. Several reference-event cards on this site say plainly that some marks were absent, and why that matters.

At that boundary, what is the operator responsible for, and what falls to government?

Where the law places defense against a state's armed forces with the state, as 10 CFR 50.13 does for nuclear facilities in the United States, reaching the military-action tier moves to the state the duty to defend the facility against its attacker. It does not move the duty to reach and hold a protected state. The facility still does that with what is on site, because the state's response is itself outside help, and outside help is exactly what an adversary denies. No design takes credit for a response by the state that it cannot show will arrive.

That is the position of FD-DBL (Design Basis Layers) §4.5 in the November 2026 Edition of the Foundational Definitions, and this page goes no further than that text.

Which dependencies lie outside any one regulator's jurisdiction?

Most of the important ones. Each sector page lists the sectors it depends on, and those are usually regulated by someone else. The water framework, for example, does not let a utility count on the power grid or chemical deliveries staying available when their loss is itself one of the events planned for (Water and Wastewater). The cascade between gas and electricity crosses two regulatory regimes by its nature (Energy).

How can a regulator judge whether an operator's claim of “enough” is enough?

Because a design basis is written in advance, the claim can be checked against stated conditions and a stated success criterion rather than against intentions. Reference events then test the method against what actually happened, and what they find goes back to revise it.
