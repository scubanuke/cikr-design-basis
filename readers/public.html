A path through the examples

For the public

These systems are easy to forget until one of them stops. The questions below are the ones most people bring, with short answers that point to the examples.

This page is a guided path through material the site already shows. It is not regulatory guidance and not a compliance checklist.

What happens to an ordinary household when one of these systems fails?

Usually more than one thing happens at once. Each sector page ends with a real event, and most of them are about ordinary people: the hospital that lost power after Hurricane Katrina (Healthcare and Public Health), the 911 outages after the derecho of June 2012 (Emergency Services), the Superdome as a refuge of last resort (Commercial Facilities), the households moved out beside the Aliso Canyon gas leak (Energy).

Read the reference-event card on any sector page. It says what happened and what the event teaches about how the facility was designed.

Why does one failure spread to others?

Because these systems lean on one another. A water system runs on electricity and on chemical deliveries. Gas keeps many power plants running, and electricity keeps much of the gas moving. Every sector page shows two lists: the sectors it depends on, and the sectors that depend on it. Follow those links and you are following the path a failure takes.

Why is designing against an adversary different from designing against a storm?

A storm does not choose its moment, does not come back for the repair crew, does not aim at the one part that matters most and does not stop help from arriving. An adversary can do all four. That changes what a good design has to achieve: not recovery with outside help, but holding a safe, protected state with only what is on site, for as long as the attack lasts. What is a design basis? explains it in a few minutes.

Should this worry me?

The point is not alarm. What we can describe, we can design for. A design basis is a promise made in advance and on the record, so that it can be checked. This site shows what such a promise looks like, sector by sector.
