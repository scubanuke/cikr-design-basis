# Energy

**So what?** Gas keeps many power plants running, and electricity keeps much of the gas moving. In a hard winter, when one falters the other can follow, and each can drag the other further down.

**Depends on:** Water, Nuclear
**Depended on by:** Water, Nuclear

## Sector framework — Energy Sector Framework (DBA-EN-SF)
A sector framework states what a sector's systems must be built to withstand, from ordinary accidents up to deliberate military action. It covers electric power, natural gas and petroleum. Gas and electricity depend on each other in both directions: gas fuels a large share of power plants, and electricity drives many of the compressors that move gas, so a failure on either side can feed on itself. The framework treats that two-way cascade as an event in its own right, analyzed from both the electric and the gas side, not as a side effect. Where an energy asset is the only supply to a high-consequence facility in another sector, the framework holds that asset to a tighter design basis.
*Source: DBA-EN-SF_Energy_Sector_Framework_v0_18*

## Facility class — Underground Storage (DBA-GAS-FC3)
A facility class takes the sector framework down to one kind of facility. This one is underground gas storage: salt caverns, depleted oil and gas fields and aquifers that store gas when demand is low for use when it is high, with the wells and compressors on the surface that reach them. Storage is the system's buffer. It is what lets a supply built for average days get through the coldest and hottest ones. The rock itself cannot be destroyed directly, but the wells that reach it can fail, and a failed well can take a field out of service for months or years. Even when such a field returns to service, it may come back at reduced capacity.
*Source: DBA-GAS-FC3_Underground_Storage_v0_10*

## Use case — Gas-Electric Cascade (DBA-EN-UC-1)
A use case runs a postulated scenario forward through the design to see where it holds and where it does not. This is a sector-wide case, written for the whole energy sector rather than one facility class. It traces the cascade between gas and electricity from its start to its end state, against the most severe real case in U.S. history, Winter Storm Uri in February 2021. The case shows that a deliberate version need not cause more physical damage than Uri did to do more lasting harm. It finds that gas and electric operators have not worked out together how they would operate through the cascade, a gap that needs coordination and exercises, not new law. This document is available to defenders and regulators on request.
*Source: DBA-EN-UC-1_Gas_Electric_Cascade_v0_2*

## Reference event — Aliso Canyon Gas Release (DBA-GAS-RE-1)
A reference event reads a real event back through the design basis to ask whether the method was sufficient. In October 2015 a leak was found at a storage well at Aliso Canyon, beside Porter Ranch in Los Angeles. Surface attempts failed; a relief well sealed it after about sixteen weeks. Thousands of households moved out temporarily, and the field's loss raised warnings of summer gas shortages to power plants. The field returned to limited operation about twenty-one months later. The instrument finds the design basis should account for the time to drill a relief well, the people beside the field, and a lasting loss of capacity. Three of the four marks were absent, so this was not military action.
*Source: DBA-GAS-RE-1_Aliso_Canyon_Gas_Release_v0_2*
