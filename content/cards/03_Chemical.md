# Chemical

**So what?** A small chemical depot you never see may be the only source of the disinfectant your water system relies on. The design looks at what happens when a supply stops, not only at what happens when something leaks or burns.

**Depends on:** Energy, Water, Transportation
**Depended on by:** Water, Food and Agriculture

## Sector framework — Chemical Sector Framework (DBA-CHEM_Sector_Framework)
A sector framework states what a sector's systems must be built to withstand, from ordinary accidents up to a deliberate, sustained attack. This sector is defined by a material rather than a service, so no single regulator governs it, and the framework sorts it by function instead: making chemicals, moving them in bulk, distributing them, and the coordination each owning company carries out for itself. Its importance to other sectors concentrates at distribution. There, a small facility can be low in hazard to its neighbors yet be the only source of a chemical that a metropolitan water system or farm region cannot do without.
*Source: DBA-CHEM_Sector_Framework_v0_11*

## Facility class — Distribution, Blending and Supply Terminals (DBA-CHEM-DI-FC3)
A facility class takes the sector framework down to one kind of facility. This one covers the terminals, repackagers, blenders and retailers that store chemicals and pass them to the people who use them. It names two harms, a release or explosion near the facility and the loss of a supply someone else depends on, and either may govern. The class sets out what a facility must do on its own when control and outside help are cut off, including that a critical supplier keep serving its critical customers. It states plainly that a sole supplier cannot be made immune to supply denial.
*Source: DBA-CHEM-DI-FC3_Distribution_Blending_and_Supply_Terminals_v0_3*

## Use case — The Sole Disinfectant Supplier Taken Down (DBA-CHEM-UC-3)
A use case runs a postulated attack forward through a facility class to see where the design holds and where it does not. Here a capable adversary takes out of service the only disinfectant supplier that a group of water utilities can reach in time. Nothing is released, yet within days the utilities run short. The case shows that the supplier's storage stays safe because its safeguards work on their own, and that the real loss is the supplier's role. The class places part of the burden on the utilities, whose own stock and alternate disinfection become the remaining protection. This document is available to defenders and regulators on request.
*Source: DBA-CHEM-UC-3_Sole_Disinfectant_Supplier_Taken_Down_v0_2*

## Reference event — Arkema Crosby Fire (DBA-CHEM-RE-2)
A reference event reads a real event back through the design basis to ask whether the method was sufficient. In August 2017, flooding from Hurricane Harvey cut power to the Arkema plant at Crosby, Texas, and defeated its emergency generators and cooling. Nearby residents were evacuated, and the stored chemicals burned over several days. The U.S. Chemical Safety Board found a common mode of failure in every layer of protection: one flood took them all. The event was not military action: intentionality, persistence and precision were absent, and denial came from the flood, not from an actor.
*Source: DBA-CHEM-RE-2_Arkema_Crosby_Fire_v0_2*
