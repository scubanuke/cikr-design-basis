# Commercial Facilities

**So what?** The stadium or arena near you may also be the shelter your community uses in a disaster. The design basis asks that a refuge be planned to sustain its people, say where overflow goes, and have its way out arranged before it opens.

**Depends on:** Energy, Communications, Transportation, Water, Financial Services
**Depended on by:** Emergency Services

## Sector framework — Commercial Facilities Sector Framework (DBA-CF_Sector_Framework)
A sector framework states what a sector's systems must be built to withstand, from ordinary accidents up to deliberate military action. This one covers places where the public gathers, stays and shops, such as stadiums, theaters, hotels and malls. Most are privately owned, open by design and governed by building and fire codes written for accidents. The framework adds two roles. Large venues serve as emergency shelters when a hurricane or flood forces evacuation, so losing one means losing a refuge when it is needed most. And some sit where several other sectors, such as rail and power, are packed closely together, so trouble there can spread across them.
*Source: DBA-CF_Sector_Framework_v0_5*

## Facility class — Public Assembly and Mass-Gathering Venues (DBA-CF-PA-FC1)
A facility class takes the sector framework down to one kind of facility. This one covers the stadium, arena, theater, convention center or event site where thousands of people gather. It follows three harms: people hurt inside the venue, the loss of the refuge when a disaster turns the venue into a shelter, and the spread of an event to the systems around it. The class sets out that a venue should not be named a refuge for a hazard it cannot withstand, that a refuge should sustain its people without relying on systems the disaster takes, and that its means of evacuation be committed before it opens as a refuge.
*Source: DBA-CF-PA-FC1_Public_Assembly_and_Mass-Gathering_Venues_v0_2*

## Use case — The Refuge Struck (DBA-CF-UC-1)
A use case runs a postulated attack forward through a facility class to see where the design holds and where it does not. Here, in a nation at war, a city far from the front opens its largest stadium as a refuge for people displaced by the fighting; the conflict takes the power and water that sustain it and the vehicles that would carry people out, and the refuge itself is struck. The case shows that capping admission with a named overflow site, and an exit plan that does not depend on the infrastructure around the venue, hold up. It also raises open questions about warning time and about evacuation vehicles the conflict itself may claim. This document is available to defenders and regulators on request.
*Source: DBA-CF-UC-1_The_Refuge_Struck_v0_1*

## Reference event — Superdome as Refuge of Last Resort in Hurricane Katrina (DBA-CF-RE-3)
A reference event reads a real event back through the design basis to ask whether the method was sufficient. In August 2005 the Louisiana Superdome served as New Orleans's refuge of last resort during Hurricane Katrina. The House committee found it lay in a floodplain and was not wind-resistant. The storm tore its roof covering and cut power and plumbing, and, on the Senate committee's figure, more than twenty thousand people stayed nearly a week. By Tuesday it was full, and about nineteen thousand gathered at the Convention Center, supplied only on Friday. There was no adversary, so the event is not a military action.
*Source: DBA-CF-RE-3_Superdome_as_Refuge_of_Last_Resort_in_Hurricane_Katrina_v0_1*
