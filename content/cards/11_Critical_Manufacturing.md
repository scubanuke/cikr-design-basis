# Critical Manufacturing

**So what?** The big machines other sectors run on, such as transformers and turbines, are built by very few plants. If both an installed machine and the capacity to replace it are lost, recovery may take years rather than months.

**Depends on:** Energy, Transportation, Chemical
**Depended on by:** Energy, Water, Communications, Transportation

## Sector framework — Critical Manufacturing Sector Framework (DBA-CM_Sector_Framework)
A sector framework states what a sector's systems must be built to withstand, from ordinary accidents up to a deliberate, sustained attack. This one covers the plants that make large components, precision parts and electronics, and strategic materials. Its main point is that the loss of a plant matters less on site than downstream, because the sectors waiting for what it makes cannot recover without it. Protecting manufacturing capacity is therefore treated as protecting the floor under every other sector's recovery. The framework names concentration in a small number of plants, many abroad, as the sector's defining exposure.
*Source: DBA-CM_Sector_Framework_v0_4*

## Facility class — Long-Lead Critical Components (DBA-CM-LL-FC1)
A facility class takes the sector framework down to one kind of facility. This one is the plant that builds large, custom components, such as power transformers and turbines, slowly and to order. It measures how long a damaged facility waits for a replacement, which depends on the order queue as much as on the build. The class asks that the queue be watched, and sets out what a reserve of spare units, planned elsewhere in the corpus, requires of the plants to bound that wait. It also states that the reserve and the number of qualified plants matter more than any one plant's protection, and that no single plant can provide them.
*Source: DBA-CM-LL-FC1_Long-Lead_Critical_Components_v0_2*

## Use case — The Asset and Its Replacement Struck Together (DBA-CM-UC-1)
A use case runs a postulated scenario forward through a facility class to see where the design holds and where it does not. Here an adversary in an armed conflict strikes a sector's installed heavy equipment and, in the same campaign, the plant that would build its replacement. The case shows that spares held beyond the campaign's reach, if they can be released when needed, can restore the highest-priority facilities. Other facilities would wait on the world's capacity to build. It also shows that a qualified source on paper is not the same as a unit delivered. This document is available to defenders and regulators on request.
*Source: DBA-CM-UC-1_The_Asset_and_Its_Replacement_Struck_Together_v0_1*

## Reference event — Denial of Large-Transformer Replacement in Ukraine (DBA-CM-RE-1)
A reference event reads a real event back through the design basis to ask whether the method was sufficient. From October 2022 repeated strikes hit Ukraine's power system. A United Nations assessment found that 42 of 94 vital high-voltage transformers were damaged or destroyed between January and April 2023, and that more than half were struck again, which blocked repair. The country's largest transformer plant was reported closed by the war in March 2022. All four marks of military action were present. The strikes are attributed to Russian armed forces by the International Criminal Court's warrants and by Ukraine and its partners. The record remains open.
*Source: DBA-CM-RE-1_Denial_of_Large_Transformer_Replacement_in_Ukraine_v0_1*
