The Cyberattack on MGM Resorts, September 2023

Event date: 11 to 20 September 2023 (record closed) · Read from instrument version 0.2 · Public Safety and Government collection

Impacted sector: Information Technology

What happened

MGM Resorts disclosed a cybersecurity issue on 11 September 2023 and shut systems down; slot machines, room keys, reservations, payments, and teller machines were unavailable or restricted for days. Its 8-K of 5 October estimated about $100 million of quarterly effect and reported customer data from before March 2019 taken. The filings do not state the entry; an ALPHV affiliate claimed it called the help desk.

Tier

Adversarial, not military action. Criminal, below the tier: intentionality and denial established, persistence not established, precision not determined.

Findings

  • F-1, One Entry Reached Years of Customers. One entry cannot reach years of customers' records.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for chains and resort groups whose properties share enterprise systems, their help desks and support contractors, and those who oversee them. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the facility class; this annex is addressed to a property or group. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions a group answers about its own properties (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account a group can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one group. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the group; it is that group’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Follow the Guidance Already Published

The company’s filings set out what it disclosed. Federal guidance on social engineering of help desks and on ransomware, and the gaming regulators’ requirements for casino systems, set out related practice. The class’s requirements (§4.4, §5.5, §7.2) state enterprise reach and manual operation, verified credential resets, and life-safety independent of the enterprise. Each is its author’s.

A.3.2 Keep Old Customer Data Out of Reach

These steps are this instrument’s, from F-1. State how long you keep customers’ personal data where your shared systems can reach it, and move or delete anything older so that a single break-in cannot take it.

A.4 For the Board

In September 2023, MGM Resorts shut down many of its computer systems after a cyberattack. For days, slot machines, digital room keys, reservations, card payments, and cash machines at its casinos and hotels were out or limited, and staff worked on paper. The company estimated about $100 million in lost earnings for the quarter and reported that personal information of some customers from before March 2019 had been taken. A criminal group claimed it got in by phoning the company’s help desk and posing as an employee; the company has not said how the attackers entered. The lessons are that systems shared by every property can all fail together, that each property needs a way to keep operating by hand, and that old customer data should not be kept where one break-in can reach it. The questions in this annex show management whether the same exposure is present in its own properties, and most of the answers cost little to find.
