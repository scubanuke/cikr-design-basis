The Failure of the First Federal COVID-19 Test Kits, February 2020

Event date: 4 to 29 February 2020, read to December 2021 (record closed) · Read from instrument version 0.2 · Public Safety and Government collection

What happened

The only authorized test for COVID-19 in early February 2020 was the CDC's, sent to public-health laboratories from 6 February. Within days many laboratories reported inconclusive results; the agency's root-cause analysis found the kits released after a check had detected a high failure rate. Laboratories able to make their own tests could use them only from 29 February, when the FDA relaxed its rule as community spread was confirmed.

Tier

No adversary established. Not an attack: a failure in making and design. None of the four characteristics is present.

Findings

  • F-1, The Second Source Was Held Back by the Rule. The second source is usable under the authority's rules from the first days of an emergency.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for public-health and clinical laboratories, the agencies that authorize tests in an emergency, and the officials who plan for the onset of an outbreak. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the facility class; this annex is addressed to a laboratory or agency. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions an organization answers about its own testing (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account an organization can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one organization. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the organization; it is that organization’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Follow the Guidance Already Published

The class’s requirements (§4.2, §5.2) state validation and lot verification before use, a second method or source, and recall and notification within hours. The Food and Drug Administration’s guidance on testing in public health emergencies, first issued on 29 February 2020 and since revised, sets out how laboratories may validate and use their own tests. Each is its author’s.

A.3.2 Have a Second Way to Test, and the Permission to Use It

These steps are this instrument’s, from F-1. Prepare a second method for detecting a new pathogen before you need it, and know under what rule you could use it in the first days of an emergency; if the rule does not allow it, say so to the authority now.

A.4 For the Board

When COVID-19 reached the United States in early 2020, the only authorized test was one made by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and sent to state public-health laboratories in early February. Within days many laboratories found it gave inconclusive results; the agency later found that the kits had been released even though a quality check had shown a high failure rate. Laboratories that could have made their own tests needed federal authorization to use them, and the rule was not relaxed until 29 February, by which time the virus was already spreading in communities. The lessons are that a test must pass its checks before it is released, and that a second way to test, and the permission to use it, must exist before the first one fails. The questions in this annex show management whether the same exposure is present in its own testing, and most of the answers cost little to find.
