Ukrainian Strikes on Russian Defense Production, 2024 and 2025

Event date: 2 April 2024 and 23 April 2025 · Read from instrument version 0.2 · Public Safety and Government collection

What happened

In April 2024 and April 2025, Ukraine's forces struck a drone plant in Russia's Alabuga special economic zone, more than 1,000 kilometers from Ukraine. In 2024 a light aircraft carrying explosives hit a workers' dormitory, while the production halls, fitted with anti-drone cages, were undamaged in imagery. The effect of the 2025 strike has not been established, and the two sides' accounts differ.

Tier

Military action, tier not established. Military action by a state's armed forces in an armed conflict, not placed at the military-action tier because denial is not established on the record.

Findings

  • F-1, Protection is designed against the campaign's weapon. The anti-drone cages over the halls held.
  • F-2, The workforce and its housing are within the campaign's reach. The strike hit the dormitory where workers live.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for defense manufacturers whose plants could be struck at long range in a conflict, wherever they stand, and for the governments that set their protective requirements. It reads a campaign against one side’s plants for what any plant’s defenders can learn from it. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the framework; this annex is addressed to an operator. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions an operator answers about the operator’s own system (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account an operator can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one operation. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the operator; it is that operation’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Protect Against the Campaign’s Weapon, Wherever the Plant Stands

No authority has published an action for owners that this instrument reads. These steps are this instrument’s, from F-1: give no credit for distance from a front; protect production buildings against the weapon a campaign would use, as the plant here fitted anti-drone cages over its main halls in 2023; plan for repeated strikes; and know the recovery time of each part of the plant.

A.3.2 Bring the Workforce Inside the Plan

This step is this instrument’s, from F-2. Include the workforce, and any housing on or near the site, in the protection plan, since the strike of 2024 hit a dormitory and not the halls.

A.4 For the Board

In April 2024 and again in April 2025, Ukraine’s forces struck a drone plant in Russia’s Alabuga special economic zone, more than 1,000 kilometers from Ukraine; the 2025 strike was claimed by Ukraine’s General Staff. The plant produces attack drones. In 2024 a single light aircraft carrying explosives hit a dormitory where students who work at the plant live; the main production halls, fitted the year before with anti-drone cages over their roofs, were undamaged in satellite imagery, and the dormitory was under repair within a week. The effect of the 2025 strike has not been established; Russia said it downed one drone. The two sides’ accounts differ, and each is stated as its own. The lessons are that distance from a front gives no protection, that a plant must be protected against the weapon a campaign uses, and that the workforce and its housing are within reach. Any defense plant that could be struck in a conflict has the same exposure. The questions in this annex show management whether this plant does, and most of the answers cost little to find.
