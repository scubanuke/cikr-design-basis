An Intelligence Officer's Attempt to Obtain Aerospace Design Data Through Engineers, 2013 to 2018

Event date: at least December 2013 to April 2018 (record closed) · Read from instrument version 0.2 · Public Safety and Government collection

What happened

On the United States government's account, a deputy division director of China's Ministry of State Security used aliases, front companies, and universities to recruit aerospace engineers to travel to China and then sought their companies' proprietary information, from 2017 seeking GE Aviation's composite fan-module design through an engineer, by then with the company and FBI cooperating. He was arrested in Belgium in 2018, convicted in 2021, and sentenced to 20 years in 2022.

Tier

Military action, tier not established. Recorded as military action below the tier: intentionality, persistence, and precision established, denial absent.

Findings

  • F-1, The Design Sought Was Outside the Class. The human path is closed where the design is held.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for defense and aerospace companies that hold design knowledge, their engineers and researchers, their security and counterintelligence staff, and those who oversee them. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the facility class; this annex is addressed to a company. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions a company answers about its own engineers (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account a company can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one company. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the company; it is that company’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Follow the Guidance Already Published

The Department of Justice’s account and the Federal Bureau of Investigation’s guidance to industry on economic espionage and insider risk set out what was found and related practice. The class’s requirements (§4.3, §5.2) state knowing who holds the design, reporting and assessing approaches, travel, and contacts, and handling an approach as an attempt on the holding. Each is its author’s.

A.3.2 Know Who Holds the Design, Wherever It Is Held

These steps are this instrument’s, from F-1. If your company holds a design with defense application, whether or not it is classified or under a defense contract, know who holds it and have their approaches reported and assessed as if it were.

A.4 For the Board

From at least 2013 until his arrest in 2018, an officer of China’s Ministry of State Security, according to the United States government, used fake names, front companies, and universities to approach engineers at aviation and aerospace companies, often by inviting them to give a talk in China, and then asked them for their companies’ secrets. One target was a GE Aviation engineer who knew the design of a composite jet-engine fan that no other company has managed to copy. As reported, the engineer went to China with company documents without telling the company. The company and the FBI ended up running the conversation, and the officer was convicted and sentenced to 20 years. The lessons are that people are the way in, and that a company must know who holds its most valuable knowledge and hear about every approach to them. The questions in this annex show management whether the same exposure is present in its own engineers, and most of the answers cost little to find.
