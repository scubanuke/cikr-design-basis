The Unauthorized Radio-Stop Broadcasts on the Polish Railways, August 2023

Event date: 25 August 2023 · Read from instrument version 0.4 · Lifelines collection

What happened

On the evening of 25 August 2023, someone without authority broadcast the emergency stop signal over the Polish railway's analog train radio near Szczecin, and every equipped train in range braked automatically. About twenty trains were delayed or halted and freight was stopped for hours; no one was hurt, and no source identifies the sender. Similar broadcasts followed across Poland over the next few days, and the railway records hundreds of unauthorized uses of the signal a year.

Tier

Adversarial, not military action. A deliberate, unauthorized act, but not military action on the record: no authority has stated that the broadcast near Szczecin was a state's act. It is an injected command on an unauthenticated channel that could carry only a stop.

Findings

  • F-1, Withdrawn. Its correction of the facility class's account of the event went to the class as an erratum.
  • F-2, A stop-only channel is abused at a rate. Unauthorized stops numbered hundreds a year, so the channel's risk should be judged at that rate, not for one event.
  • F-3, A stop is not harmless everywhere. A sudden stop is safe in most places but not for a heavy train on a grade or a train on a bridge.
  • F-4, Migration is not closure. The replacement digital system is years late, and the old channel stays open where it is not yet in service.
  • F-5, The proceed after the stop travels on the same channel. The instruction to restart is given by voice on the same open radio, and should be protected.
  • F-6, The reach is the network's, and closing it is not the railway's alone. The same design runs on every line, and finding a transmitter depends on the spectrum regulator and the police.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for railways and other operators whose vehicles can be commanded over a radio channel, above all analog train radio with an emergency stop function, and for the spectrum regulators and police who can find a transmitter. It is also for any owner, in any sector, whose safety function can be triggered by anyone with a transmitter. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the framework; this annex is addressed to an operator. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions an operator answers about the operator’s own system (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account an operator can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the operator; it is that operation’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Know Who Can Command Your Vehicles, and How Often They Do

This step is this instrument’s, from F-2 and F-6. Know every channel by which a command reaches your vehicles and whether anyone outside can use it; count its unauthorized uses as a rate, in traffic stopped a year, not only as incidents; and count the exposure across every line that shares the same design.

A.3.2 Plan for the Stop Where It Hurts, and Protect the Restart

These steps are this instrument’s, from F-3 and F-5. Name the places and loads at which a sudden stop carries consequence beyond delay, and judge the channel’s risk for them; and deliver the instruction to proceed after an injected stop over an authenticated channel, or confirm it by a means the sender cannot reach.

A.3.3 Close the Old Channel, Not Just Build the New One

Poland’s Ministry of Infrastructure has stated that each case is reported to the Office of Electronic Communications, the Railway Protection Service, and the police, who try to locate the transmitter; that dual-system radios at the traffic posts allow the signal to be coded; and that a modified alarm signal harder to imitate is to be studied and deployed after tests. PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe accelerated the replacement of analog train radios with dual-system radios. These steps add the instrument’s own, from F-4 and F-6: measure migration as the share of your network on which the old channel is closed to outside transmitters, not the share on which the new one exists; credit a modified signal only once it is shown to authenticate; and agree in advance with the spectrum regulator and the police how a transmitter will be found.

A.4 For the Board

On the evening of 25 August 2023, someone without authority broadcast the emergency stop signal over the Polish railway’s analog train radio near Szczecin, and every equipped train in range braked automatically. About twenty trains were delayed or halted and freight was stopped for hours; no one was hurt, and no source identifies the sender. Similar broadcasts followed across Poland over the next few days. The old radio system lets anyone with a transmitter on general sale send the stop, and the railway has recorded hundreds of such uses a year, more than a thousand in 2024 on its count as reported. Because the channel could carry only a stop, the worst an outsider could do was halt trains, not send them into danger, which is why no one was hurt. But a sudden stop is not harmless everywhere, the instruction to restart travels by voice on the same open channel, and the replacement digital system is years late and, on the railway’s own list, does not yet cover the lines involved. No authority has stated that the broadcast near Szczecin was a state’s act. The lessons are that a channel anyone can reach will be used at a rate, not once, and that building a new system does not close the old one. Any operation whose vehicles can be commanded over a channel outsiders can reach has the same exposure. The questions in this annex show management whether this operation does, and most of the answers cost little to find.
