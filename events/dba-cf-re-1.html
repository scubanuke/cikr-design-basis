The Bombing at the Manchester Arena, May 2017

Event date: 22 May 2017 · Read from instrument version 0.2 · Public Safety and Government collection

What happened

At 22:31 on 22 May 2017, as a concert audience was leaving, a bomb was detonated in the foyer linking the Manchester Arena to Manchester Victoria station, outside the screened perimeter. Twenty-two people were killed and many hundreds injured. The public inquiry found that the attacker had reconnoitered the venue, waited partly out of camera view, and was the subject of a concern that never reached senior security, and that no officer was in the foyer as the crowd came through.

Tier

Adversarial, not military action. A deliberate attack, not military action: the inquiry found no state acted.

Findings

  • F-1, The watch on the edge is confirmed when it is needed. No officer was in the foyer when the crowd came through it.
  • F-2, A concern at the edge has a time to reach someone who acts. A member of the public's concern reached stewards and went no further.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for arenas, stadiums, theaters, and other venues where large crowds gather, for the security contractors and transport police who share their approaches, and for the stewards who are often the first to see something wrong. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the framework; this annex is addressed to an operator. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions an operator answers about the operator’s own system (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account an operator can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one operation. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the operator; it is that operation’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Watch the Edge When the Crowd Passes, and Settle the Shared Space

The Manchester Arena Inquiry found that the loss of life and injury would highly likely have been less had the attacker been confronted, and recommended a statutory duty on those responsible for premises to prepare for and protect against terrorist attack, with those who share a space bearing responsibility for its common areas in proportion to their use. The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 placed that duty on qualifying premises and events in the United Kingdom, with the Security Industry Authority as regulator. These steps add the instrument’s own, from F-1: confirm on the night that the watch on the edge is in place when the crowd passes.

A.3.2 Give a Concern a Time to Reach Someone Who Acts

This step is this instrument’s, from F-2. State how quickly a concern raised at the edge must reach someone with authority to act, and train stewards and staff to pass it on.

A.4 For the Board

At 22:31 on 22 May 2017, as a concert audience was leaving the Manchester Arena, a man detonated a bomb in the foyer linking the arena to Manchester Victoria railway station, outside the arena’s screened perimeter. Twenty-two people were killed and many hundreds injured. The public inquiry found that he had carried out reconnaissance on at least three occasions, that he waited in the foyer partly where cameras did not see, that a member of the public’s concern about him reached stewards but not senior security management, and that the three organizations responsible for the foyer did not coordinate and no officer was there when the crowd came through. It found that had he been confronted, the loss would highly likely have been less, and the United Kingdom later enacted a duty on venues to prepare for and protect against terrorist attack. No state was involved. The lessons are that the edge of a venue must be watched when the crowd passes, that a concern must reach someone who acts in time, and that those who share a space must settle who covers it. Any venue that holds large crowds has the same exposure. The questions in this annex show management whether this venue does, and most of the answers cost little to find.
