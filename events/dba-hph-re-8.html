The Ransomware Attack on Synnovis, June 2024

Event date: 3 June 2024 onward, read to June 2025 (record closed as to the disruption) · Read from instrument version 0.2 · Public Safety and Government collection

What happened

On 3 June 2024 ransomware disabled Synnovis, the pathology provider to hospitals across south-east London. Testing and blood matching ran at about a tenth of normal in the second week; more than ten thousand appointments and seventeen hundred procedures were postponed, and one trust later said the attack contributed to a death. Hospitals used O-type blood, and NHS Blood and Transplant issued an amber alert for O-negative in July. A criminal group claimed the attack.

Tier

Adversarial, not military action. A criminal ransomware attack; not placed at the military-action tier: persistence and precision are not established and denial is absent.

Findings

  • F-1, The Manual Mode Ran at a Tenth of Need. The manual mode is sized against urgent need, and the exercise shows it.
  • F-2, The Substitution Drew Down the Nation's Universal Blood. The reserve a substitution draws on is counted, with the time it lasts.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for hospitals and health systems that rely on a shared or outsourced laboratory, the laboratories themselves, and the blood services that supply them. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the facility class; this annex is addressed to a hospital or laboratory. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions an organization answers about its own testing (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account an organization can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one organization. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the organization; it is that organization’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Follow the Guidance Already Published

The class’s requirements (§3.3, §4.5, §5.3) state the share counted across every hospital served, a manual mode exercised in advance, results released only by a trusted path, and restoration from beyond the attack’s reach. NHS Blood and Transplant’s guidance on the use of O-negative blood and its amber-alert procedures set out how the blood supply is managed in a shortage. Each is its author’s.

A.3.2 Size Your Manual Mode, and Tell Your Blood Service What It Would Draw

These steps are this instrument’s, from F-1 and F-2. Work out how much urgent testing and blood matching you must keep doing if your laboratory’s systems are lost, and exercise your manual mode at that rate. And tell your blood service how much universal blood you would use per day if you could not match, so that the reserve is planned and not discovered.

A.4 For the Board

On 3 June 2024 a ransomware attack disabled Synnovis, the company that runs blood tests and blood matching for hospitals across south-east London. The hospitals fell back on manual methods that, in the second week, ran at about a tenth of normal. Thousands of operations and appointments were postponed, and one hospital trust later said the attack contributed to a patient’s death. Unable to match blood, the hospitals used universal type O blood, and a month later the national blood service declared a severe shortage of O-negative. The lessons are that a backup way of testing must be sized to what patients need, not just written down, and that a hospital’s fallback can quietly drain a national reserve unless it is planned with the blood service. The questions in this annex show management whether the same exposure is present in its own testing, and most of the answers cost little to find.
