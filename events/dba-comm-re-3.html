The Tonga Isolation, January 2022

Event date: 15 January 2022 · Read from instrument version 0.5 · Lifelines collection

What happened

On 15 January 2022, the eruption of the submarine volcano Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha'apai sent currents of debris along the seabed that broke both of Tonga's cables: its single international cable and the domestic cable to its northern islands. The main island was on limited satellite links for thirty-eight days, the northern islands waited eighteen months for their cable, and banks, exports, and remittances stopped. The repair ship came from nearly 5,000 kilometers away, and spare cable had to be borrowed.

Tier

No adversary established. Not a deliberate act: a natural-hazard event, a volcanic eruption that cut both of a region's paths at once, below the threshold for the military-action tier.

Findings

  • F-1, A natural hazard is sized by its reach and the length it destroys. The eruption's currents ran more than 100 kilometers on the seabed and destroyed more than 89 kilometers of cable.
  • F-2, Repair time and repair capacity are set by the ship's position and the cable in stock. The ship's distance and a shortage of spare cable set the thirty-eight days and the eighteen months.
  • F-3, Withdrawn. Merged into F-2.
  • F-4, The surviving service is held in reserve, not bought after. Satellite capacity met about an eighth of demand, and new paths came only after the cable was restored.
  • F-5, Withdrawn. Its substance is recorded as context and was taken in by the class.
  • F-6, A nation's path count is counted island by island. The northern islands had one cable to their capital and were cut off for eighteen months.
  • F-7, A sole path lost once is a residual realized. Tonga lost its sole international path in 2019 and again in 2022, and a second cable came only in 2026.
  • F-8, A second path is counted from the dependents and against the hazard. A second cable credits only the places its dependents are, and only against hazards it does not share.
  • F-9, Emergency communications without the backbone are in place before the event. The government's first statement took three days, and equipment flown in was quarantined.
  • F-10, Withdrawn. Its correction of the facility class's account of the event was taken in by the class.

For asset owners

Annex A of the instrument, in full. It is written for owners and operators, and it states no requirement.

A.1 What This Annex Is

This annex is for island states and remote regions, the operators of their cables and satellite links, and the governments, banks, and emergency services that depend on them. It is also for any owner, in any sector, whose connection to the outside world rests on one path. It is not a finding and states no requirement. The recommendations of Section 8 are addressed to the framework; this annex is addressed to an operator. It has three parts, each drawn from a section of the instrument: a self-check that turns the event’s elements (Table 4-1) into questions an operator answers about the operator’s own system (A.2); the actions published, each stated as its publisher’s, with this instrument’s own marked and tied to a finding (A.3); and a plain-language account an operator can give a board (A.4).

A.2 Would It Happen Here?

The instrument reads the event against the design basis. The self-check reads the same event against one operation. Each question is keyed to the element of Table 4-1 it comes from. A “no” or an “I don’t know” is not a failure of the operator; it is that operation’s finding, and the place to start.

A.3 What to Do Now

A.3.1 Count Your Paths Island by Island, and Against the Hazard

These steps are this instrument’s, from F-1, F-6, F-7, and F-8. Count your paths to the outside world and from each populated place to those it depends on; size your exposure to a natural hazard by its reach and the length of cable it could destroy; credit a second path only for the places its dependents are, and only against hazards it does not share; and where you have lost a sole path once, record the basis on which you accept a path count of one.

A.3.2 Know Your Repair Ship and Your Spares

This step is this instrument’s, from F-2. Know where your repair ship is and how long it would take to reach you, and hold spare cable of every type you use in the lengths a major event could destroy; in 2022 the ship came from nearly 5,000 kilometers away, and the operator borrowed spare cable held for another customer.

A.3.3 Hold the Surviving Service, and Emergency Communications, Before the Event

No authority has published an action for owners from this event; after it, Tonga’s operators agreed a satellite backhaul and a standby terminal, and a second international cable was switched on in 2026. These steps are this instrument’s, from F-4 and F-9: hold standby capacity in reserve, licensed and tested, sized to the traffic that matters, including payments and the coordination of a disaster response; and keep emergency communications that do not depend on the backbone in the country, licensed and practiced, with arrangements so that equipment sent in is not held at the border.

A.4 For the Board

On 15 January 2022, the eruption of the submarine volcano Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha’apai broke both of the Kingdom of Tonga’s cables: the single international cable that joined it to the world, and the domestic cable that joined its capital to its northern islands. Currents of volcanic debris ran along the seabed for more than 100 kilometers and destroyed more than 89 kilometers of the international cable. The main island was on limited satellite links for thirty-eight days, the government’s first statement came three days after the eruption, and banks, exports, and remittances stopped. The repair ship came from nearly 5,000 kilometers away, and the operator had to borrow spare cable; the northern islands waited eighteen months for their cable. Both cables had been cut before, in 2019, and the domestic cable was cut again in 2024. The lessons are that a sole path will be lost, more than once, and that the service that survives it must be in place before the event, not bought after it. Any place whose connection to the outside world rests on one path has the same exposure. The questions in this annex show management whether this operation does, and most of the answers cost little to find.
