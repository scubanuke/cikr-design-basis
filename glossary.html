Terms in plain language

Glossary

These are the terms the site uses, in plain language. A dotted underline anywhere on the site marks a term defined here; tap it to see the definition. The authoritative definitions are in the Foundational Definitions, and where the two differ, those govern.

Adversary
An actor who sets out to defeat what a facility is built to do, or the requirement that it do it safely. An adversary is defined by what it can do, not by who it is.
Deliberate military action
The layer of analysis that adds an adversary showing all four marks: intentionality, persistence, precision and denial. What counts as success changes: the facility must reach and hold a protected state using only what is at the facility, for as long as the attack lasts.
Denial
The adversary blocks the repair and outside help the response depends on: access to the site, resupply, outside emergency response and communications.
Design basis
A written statement, made in advance and on the record, of what a facility must be able to withstand and what counts as success when it is tested. Because it is written down beforehand, anyone can check it, and everyone can tell when it has been exceeded.
Design basis accident
The layer of analysis that adds the disruptive events experience has taught us to expect: fire, flood, storm, failed equipment, an operator's error, a flawed process. The facility must keep working, or reach a safe, known condition and recover from it with outside help.
Edition
A dated, frozen set of the documents this site links to, deposited under one DOI so that what a reader cites does not change when the working documents do. Edition 1 is dated 1 November 2026.
Facility class
One kind of facility within a sector, such as a water treatment plant or a 911 dispatch center, with the sector framework taken down to that kind of facility.
Intentionality
The adversary chooses what happens, when and in what order, watches the response and adapts to defeat it. A storm does not.
Operating limits
The limits within which a facility is run so that it stays inside its design basis, with the responses and reports that keep it there.
Persistence
The adversary keeps the pressure on, or repeats it, so the event does not end on its own. The facility must keep operating safely for as long as it lasts.
Precision
The adversary aims at the parts whose loss is hardest to recover from, so failures that would otherwise be chance happen together by design.
Protected state
The condition a facility reaches and holds under attack, keeping the harm bounded with only the resources on site, for as long as the attack lasts. The facility may stop doing its job; it never stops doing it safely.
Reference event
A real event read back through the design basis to ask whether the method was sufficient: what actually happened, as opposed to what we postulate. What it finds goes back to revise the design basis.
Sector framework
The document that sets the design basis for a whole sector: what the sector's systems must be built to withstand, from ordinary accidents up to deliberate military action, and which functions must hold.
The four marks
Intentionality, persistence, precision and denial. An adversary at the military-action layer shows all four. When a real event is read back, it counts as military action only if all four are established on the record; a mark that cannot be shown counts as absent.
Use case
A postulated scenario run forward through the design to see where it holds and where it does not: what we anticipate, as opposed to what happened. On this site every use case is shown as a summary card only.

Every document linked from this site is frozen in CIKR Design Basis Examples, Edition 1 (November 2026), doi:10.5281/zenodo.23863986, as it stood on 1 November 2026. Each is a working draft, marked DRAFT on its cover. The working documents continue to change, and later editions will record them.
